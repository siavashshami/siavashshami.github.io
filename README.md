**About**

Siavash Shami is a geodesist and InSAR researcher specializing in surface deformation monitoring, geohazards, and Earth Sciences. He received his M.Sc. in Geodesy from K. N. Toosi University of Technology in 2018, where his thesis focused on InSAR applications in open‑pit mines. His research integrates InSAR, GNSS, and satellite remote sensing with geological and hydrological data to investigate natural and anthropogenic deformation, including earthquakes, landslides, salt‑dome displacement, and infrastructure monitoring. He also applies machine learning and deep learning to deformation modeling and environmental remote sensing. A parallel focus of his research is on the technical and methodological aspects of InSAR, including error characterization, phase bias, atmospheric corrections, and interferogram network optimization, as well as the application of remote sensing data to study a wide range of Earth Sciences phenomena, from groundwater dynamics to land cover change and soil erosion. He has authored 7 peer‑reviewed international journal articles published in journals indexed in Web of Science (WoS) across five major publishers: Elsevier, Springer Nature, IEEE, Taylor & Francis, and MDPI. He has also served as a peer reviewer for multiple WoS‑indexed journals and has authored practical handbooks on InSAR processing.
